Dry Waste

The recyclable backbone of your trash — giving materials a second life through reuse and recovery.

What is Dry Waste?

Dry waste includes non-biodegradable, recyclable materials that don't contain moisture. It's the stuff that can be processed and reborn into new products, conserving resources and energy.

Common Examples

  • Paper & cardboard (newspapers, boxes, books)
  • Plastics (bottles, bags, containers)
  • Metals (cans, foils, wires)
  • Glass (bottles, jars)
  • Rubber & leather items
  • Old clothes, shoes, & fabrics
  • E-waste (batteries, wires, small electronics)

Why Segregate It?

  • Enables recycling, saving raw materials like trees, oil, and metals
  • Reduces energy consumption — recycling aluminum saves 95% energy
  • Prevents landfill overflow and pollution from non-degradable items
  • Creates jobs in the recycling industry and supports circular economy

How to Handle Dry Waste

  • Rinse and dry items to remove residues
  • Flatten boxes and crush bottles to save space
  • Use a separate dry bin away from moisture
  • Sell to local recyclers or hand over to collection services
  • Never burn or mix with wet waste — it ruins recyclables
PRO TIP
Upcycle your dry waste — turn old bottles into planters or newspapers into crafts to reduce waste even further.
