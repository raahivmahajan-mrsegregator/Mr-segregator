Sanitary Waste

Personal hygiene waste that requires careful handling to protect health and the environment.

What is Sanitary Waste?

Sanitary waste includes used personal hygiene products that may contain bodily fluids. These items are non-recyclable and need special care to prevent contamination and health risks.

Common Examples

  • Sanitary napkins, pads & tampons
  • Baby diapers & adult diapers
  • Condoms & intimate hygiene products
  • Bandages, gauze & cotton with blood
  • Medical gloves & masks (household use)
  • Wet wipes & tissues with fluids

Why Segregate It?

  • Prevents spread of infections and diseases
  • Avoids contaminating recyclable dry waste
  • Reduces environmental pollution from plastics
  • Ensures safe disposal through proper incineration or treatment

Health Risks of Improper Handling

  • Exposure to bloodborne pathogens like hepatitis B, C, or HIV from contaminated items
  • Respiratory infections from mishandled masks or tissues carrying viruses
  • Skin irritations or bacterial infections (e.g., E. coli, Salmonella) for waste handlers
  • Groundwater contamination leading to community health issues if landfilled untreated
  • Increased risk of vector-borne diseases if mixed waste attracts pests

How to Handle Sanitary Waste

  • Wrap each item securely in newspaper or a black bag
  • Never flush — it clogs drains and harms waterways
  • Use a separate sealed sanitary bin
  • Hand over only to designated collectors
  • Never mix with wet or dry waste
PRO TIP
Always wrap sanitary products properly before disposal — it maintains dignity, hygiene, and helps waste collectors.
