River Pollution

Every day, over 800 million litres of largely untreated sewage flows into the Yamuna River, along with another 44 million litres of industrial effluent.

More than 800 million litres of largely untreated sewage is pumped into the Yamuna each day, plus another 44 million litres of industrial effluents [1]. India generates around 62 million tonnes of scattered municipal solid waste annually [2], with a significant fraction leaking directly into aquatic ecosystems. (Earth5R)

Unsegregated waste is a major threat to rivers because it often ends up in drains, canals, and waterways. Plastic packaging, food waste, paper, and other discarded materials can be carried by rainwater and drainage systems into rivers, where they accumulate and pollute the water. As this waste breaks down, it can release harmful substances and microplastics, affecting aquatic plants, animals, and the wider food chain.

Unsegregated waste can also collect along riverbanks and obstruct the natural flow of water. Organic waste can decompose in the water and reduce dissolved oxygen levels, creating aquatic “dead zones” that are harmful to fish and other aquatic organisms. Plastic waste and other debris can block waterways, increasing the risk of waterlogging and flooding during heavy rainfall. Polluted water sources can also create conditions where disease-causing organisms can thrive.

The pollution of the Yamuna does not only affect the river itself; it also threatens the people, animals, and communities that depend on it. When waste is improperly disposed of, pollutants can travel through drains and waterways, making their way into rivers and surrounding ecosystems. By properly segregating waste at the source, we can reduce the amount of plastic, organic waste, and other materials that reach our rivers. Recycling and responsible disposal can help protect aquatic life and contribute to cleaner and healthier waterways.
